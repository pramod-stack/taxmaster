Income Tax

How to file an ITR in Bangalore

Filing your income tax return follows the same sequence whether you do it yourself or hand it to a professional. Identify your income sources — salary, interest, rent, capital gains, freelance or business income — because they decide which ITR form applies to you. Then gather the year’s papers: Form 16 from your employer, your Form 26AS and AIS summaries, bank statements and interest certificates.

Report each income under its correct head, claim the deductions you can substantiate, reconcile the tax deducted against Form 26AS, and file on the portal — verifying the return with Aadhaar OTP, which is what actually completes the process. Keep the acknowledgement with your records; it is the proof you filed.

Simple salary returns are manageable on your own. Returns with capital gains, freelance income, multiple properties or foreign income reward a professional review — the cost of an error usually exceeds the fee.

Can I fix a mistake after filing? Yes — a belated return can be filed after the deadline, and a revised return can correct a filed one within the permitted window. If you spot an error, revise promptly rather than waiting for a notice.

Documents required for ITR filing

The core set for most individuals: PAN, Aadhaar, Form 16 from each employer, your Form 26AS and AIS, bank statements, and interest certificates from banks, FDs and post office deposits.

Situations add documents: capital gains statements from your broker or AMC when you sold shares or funds; rent receipts and landlord PAN when claiming HRA; investment proofs when claiming deductions; and for business or freelance income, your books of account and invoice records. You don’t attach documents while filing — but you must keep them, because the department can ask for support later.

Do I need to upload Form 16 while filing? No — the ITR is filed without attachments. Form 16 and the other papers are the basis of your numbers and stay with you as supporting records.

Old vs new tax regime

The two regimes are different bargains. The new regime offers lower tax rates but strips out most deductions — beyond the standard deduction, your salary income is largely taxed as-is. The old regime keeps rates higher but rewards deductions: eligible investments, insurance, home loan interest, HRA and similar claims reduce your taxable income.

The right answer depends entirely on your numbers — income mix, home loan, family situation and the deductions you can actually substantiate. The sensible approach is a side-by-side comparison using your real figures, done once a year before you finalise investments and again before filing. Salaried taxpayers can generally revisit the choice each year, while business income carries restrictions on switching — confirm the current rules for your case.

Which regime saves more tax? There is no universal answer — at moderate incomes with heavy deductions the old regime can win; with few deductions the new regime usually does. Compare with actual numbers; that comparison is a standard part of our tax planning consultation.

Tax notice: what should you do?

First — don’t panic. Most notices today are automated: a mismatch between your return and the tax deducted against your PAN, a high-value transaction reported by a bank or mutual fund, or a campaign message asking you to review your filing. Very few are audits.

Read three things: the section under which it was issued, what it is actually asking for, and the response deadline. Reconcile the claim against your own records, respond within the timeline through the proper channel, and keep proof of what you filed. If the notice involves scrutiny, reopening or amounts you don’t understand, get professional help before responding — an incorrect reply can convert a query into a liability.

Is receiving a notice a punishment? No. Notices are how the department communicates — many simply ask you to verify or explain. What matters is responding correctly and on time.

GST

GST registration guide

GST registration becomes mandatory once your aggregate turnover crosses the threshold that applies to your goods or services and your state’s category — and it is required from day one for sellers on e-commerce platforms, with special provisions for certain services. Businesses below the threshold sometimes register voluntarily to claim input tax credit or to serve B2B clients who expect a GSTIN.

The application needs PAN, Aadhaar, a photograph, premises proof with an owner consent or rent agreement where applicable, bank proof and, for entities, the incorporation documents. After submission and Aadhaar authentication, the officer either approves or raises a query — queries usually concern premises documents, which is where prepared applications pay off. Once registered, invoicing rules, HSN codes and your filing calendar apply immediately.

Does GST registration cost anything officially? The government charges no fee for registration — professionals charge for preparing the file correctly and handling queries. Beware of ‘free registration’ offers that recover costs elsewhere.

GST return filing explained

Regular taxpayers typically file two returns per cycle: GSTR-1, which reports your outward supplies invoice-wise, and GSTR-3B, the summary return where tax is actually paid. Your GSTR-1 feeds your customers’ input credit — which is why accuracy there protects your client relationships too.

Smaller taxpayers can opt for the QRMP scheme — quarterly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B with monthly tax payments — while composition dealers file a different set entirely (CMP-08 and GSTR-4). Many businesses also owe an annual return (GSTR-9). Whatever the cycle, the discipline is the same: reconcile your sales records against what you file, pay by the due date to avoid interest, and keep an audit trail of filings and challans.

What happens if I miss a GST due date? Late fees apply per return, interest applies on unpaid tax, and pending returns block further filing — your customers’ credit also gets affected. Missed periods are catchable; bring them to a professional promptly.

Common GST mistakes to avoid

The recurring ones we see in notices: invoices with the wrong HSN code or tax rate; input tax credit claimed without a valid tax invoice — or on credits blocked by law; returns never reconciled against purchase records, so credit claimed doesn’t match GSTR-2B; filings done late, stacking fees and interest; and business receipts running through personal bank accounts, which destroys the paper trail.

None of these are exotic — they are habit problems. A clean invoicing template, a monthly reconciliation, and a filing calendar shared with a professional prevent almost all of them.

I already made a mistake in a filed return — what now? Depending on what it is, corrections happen through amendments in later returns, credit reversals, or a reply to the department. The right fix depends on the error — bring it over early, while the correction windows are open.

Business

Proprietorship vs LLP

A proprietorship is you, trading: simplest to start, taxed at your personal slab, but with no legal separation — business liabilities are personal liabilities, and the business’s continuity is tied to you.

An LLP is a separate legal entity: liability is limited to the firm, partners can join and leave by agreement, and it carries moderate compliance — incorporation, annual filings and an audit once turnover crosses limits. It suits professional firms, family businesses and ventures with multiple owners who don’t plan to raise equity investment. Choose by asking: what happens to this business — and to me — if it takes on debt or a dispute?

Can I convert my proprietorship into an LLP later? Yes — conversions are possible, with formalities around assets, GST registration and the partnership changeover. It is usually easier to start with the structure you’ll need at scale.

LLP vs Private Limited Company

A Private Limited Company is built for growth capital: shares, ESOPs, investor-readiness and the strongest credibility with banks and vendors — in exchange for the heaviest compliance load: mandatory audits, board processes, annual ROC filings and higher professional costs every year, from the first rupee of turnover.

An LLP delivers limited liability and a separate legal identity with far lighter routine compliance and flexible profit-sharing — but it cannot issue equity to investors. A practical rule of thumb: raising investment or building a startup-shaped company points to Private Limited; a professional practice or family business points to LLP. Confirm the current compliance requirements for each before deciding — they change.

Which is cheaper to run year to year? An LLP generally carries lower recurring compliance cost, while a company’s costs are more front-loaded and uniform. Get a realistic first-three-years comparison before choosing — not just the registration fee.

Starting a business in Bangalore — the setup sequence

A sensible order: decide the structure first — it drives everything else. Register the entity (incorporation for companies and LLPs, deed and registrations for partnerships and proprietorships), obtain PAN/TAN where applicable, then add the registrations your activity actually triggers: GST, Udyam for MSME benefits, FSSAI for food, Karnataka Professional Tax and the Shops and Establishments registration for premises.

Open a current account in the business’s name, set up compliant invoicing, and put a compliance calendar on the wall — return due dates, advance tax dates, and renewal dates. Most early-stage trouble comes from doing these out of order, not from doing them badly.

A local professional sequences this for you and leaves you with a calendar — that is most of what ‘business setup’ means.

How much does a business setup cost? It depends on the structure and the registrations involved — a proprietorship setup and a company incorporation are different scopes. Ask for a written scope and quote before starting.

Accounting & Compliance

Bookkeeping basics for small business

Four habits carry most small businesses’ books: keep a separate bank account for the business; record every receipt and payment weekly — not at year-end; keep every purchase bill, because it supports both GST credit and expense claims; and reconcile the books against the bank statement monthly, so errors surface while they’re small.

From those habits come the three numbers to check monthly: what customers owe you, what you owe suppliers, and what to set aside for GST and tax. A business that knows those three numbers is rarely surprised by a filing season.

Do I need accounting software from day one? No — structured, complete records matter more than the tool. Start simple, and move to software when transaction volume justifies it; your records should transfer cleanly when you do.

TDS basics for business owners

TDS — tax deducted at source — applies when your business makes specified payments: salaries, rent, contractor and professional payments, commissions and more, each with its own rate and threshold set by law. The business becomes a ‘deductor’: it needs a TAN, must deduct the right amount at the right time, deposit it by the due date, file quarterly TDS returns, and issue certificates to the people it deducted from.

The compliance traps are procedural: missing a deduction, depositing late (interest applies), or failing to file the quarterly return — which can attract fees and, for expense claims, scrutiny. If you have payments that might attract TDS, a short review sets up the right process.

I forgot to deduct TDS on a payment — what now? Regularise it: deduct now, pay with interest, and check whether a penalty exposure exists — the position improves the earlier you act. Bring the specifics to a professional before the next quarterly return.
